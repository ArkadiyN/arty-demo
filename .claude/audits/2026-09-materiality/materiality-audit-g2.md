## Patterns

- **The signal that separated C from B before the outcome was known was the finding's *kind*, not its size.** Every C (and most A) findings named a basis mismatch: wrong denominator (/779 pit census vs /700 panel perforation), wrong caliber's mass spectrum weighting c, two moments averaged over different populations, a demo-regime constant (l/x0=20) used for real shells. Every B finding was wording, label provenance, stale "before" figures, or a choice between branches that both landed on the same side of the decision edge. "Is this the same quantity, population, regime, or criterion?" works as a screen before anything is computed. "How big is it?" does not.
- **Distance to the threshold predicts materiality; relative size does not.** 09-22 F4 dismissed a ~15% stale pair as "within the 2x band" when the move from 2.00 to 1.70 crossed the band edge. kappa-x B2 was a 12% move on a value sitting at 2.00. In the other direction, kappa-x B1 (a 6.5% move on mu, count arm 1.78x vs 1.89x) was correctly immaterial, because both branches sat on the same side of 2.0. A gate has to ask "does either branch cross a declared decision threshold?". It also has to be declared on the output that carries the verdict: kappa-x B1 fails the parameter-level +/-3% kappa_x budget but passes every output-level tolerance.
- **Unquantified is not small.** H1 was tagged deferrable because "a finding without a quantified impact cannot block". It suggested "a few %" of caliber-transfer error, and it came just before the cluster's largest reversal (A1: at 75 mm c = 0.97 not 1.25, PASS 1.88x became FAIL 2.30x). The falloff arm (count-gap F1) likewise had no delta at all until someone ran it. A gate must force a U finding to a bound, not pass it through as below tolerance.
- **Self-checks on the calibration case hide transfer failures.** c agreed to 0.6% at 155 mm, the caliber it was measured on, and the B(r) cross-check ran on the same caliber family. Both passed while 75 mm reversed sign. A tolerance gate evaluated only where the data lives would have filtered A1 wrongly.
- **Most of the waste came from re-synchronising the same published verdict, not from chasing residual shifts.** The count-gap verdict was re-closed and re-bannered through six banner generations: 2.25 -> 2.28 -> 2.00/2.22 -> 1.89/1.70 -> compound FAIL. That happened once per upstream constant (c, k, kappa_x). Around it went R1/R2-style historiography and a parser trip on marker text quoted in prose. Actual small-residual chases were rare and reviewers mostly labelled them immaterial themselves: N 1756 vs 1757 (0.06%), kappa_x MC seed digits (+0.22%), D4's 1.3% stale "before" figure. None changed anything. A materiality gate would trim the historiography cycles safely. The larger saving is ordering: re-close downstream verdicts once, after the constant family settles, with a grep for retired labels in place of a review cycle.
