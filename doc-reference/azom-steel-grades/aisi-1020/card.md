# AISI 1020 Steel — Card

**Source:** AZoM article (ArticleID=6114)

## Composition

- **Carbon:** 0.17–0.23% (low-carbon steel; well below the 0.45% of AISI 1045)
- **Manganese:** 0.30–0.60%
- **Silicon:** not specified
- **Phosphorus:** ≤0.040%
- **Sulfur:** ≤0.050%
- **Iron:** balance (99.08–99.53%)

## Mechanical Properties (§ Mechanical Properties)

- **Yield Strength (YS):** 294.74 MPa (42,748 psi)
- **Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS):** 394.72 MPa (57,249 psi)
- **Hardness (Brinell):** 111 HB
- **Elongation at Break:** 36.5% (significantly higher ductility than AISI 1045)
- **Reduction of Area:** 66.0%
- **Charpy Impact:** 16.9–68.0 J
- **Izod Impact:** 125 J
- **Modulus of Elasticity:** 200 GPa (29,000 ksi)

## Physical Properties

- **Density:** 7.87 g/cc

## Characteristics & Applications

- **High machinability, high strength, high ductility, good weldability**
- Resists induction and flame hardening due to low carbon
- Responds to carburization for case hardening (Rc > 65)
- Used for shafts, gears, fasteners, and general structural components

## Notes

- **War Department (WD) nomenclature:** NOT mentioned in source
- **WD-1020 designation:** No historical WD designation found

**Candidate for "WD-1020"?** Composition and properties are consistent with low-carbon AISI 1020, but no WD nomenclature is documented in this reference.
