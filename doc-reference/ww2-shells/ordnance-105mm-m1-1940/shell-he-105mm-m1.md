GENE RAL INFORMATION
In addition to this Description of Manufacture, a contractor should be provided with a set of speci­
fications for this shell, and a set of regular drawings. Drawings of the finished shell and forging are in­
cluded in this description but should be checked carefully to see that they are the latest revised copies. If
the revision dates check, these drawings should enable a manufacturer to proceed satisfactorily in default of
the regular set. If the revision dates do not check, all tools and gages contained herein pertaining to the
revised dimensions should be changed to suit.
In the manufacture of this shell there are certain considerations which are particularly Important
for personnel to bear in mind:
In the first place, it is essential that the forgings which are furnished be free from porosity par­
tlcularly in the base; that they be concentric within limits and that they have been properly cooled or nor­
malized in order not to present hard spots when they come to be machined. It is important also to note that
ln the turning of all shell, such turning is done with reference to the inside rather than the outside. This
Improves the concentricity which is highly Important. Very good results have been obtained by the use of
sintered or cemented carbide tools of the proper grade, and their use for turning operations is recommended in
machines that are capable of the necessary speed. In general, the ruggedness of the machines to Insure accur-
acy is most essential, and it is highly recommended that the weights of the machine tools for the various oper­
ations listed be ample
The rotating band should be pressed into its groove under sufficient pressure to Insure tight seat-
lng but not great enough to overwork the metal of the band and thus cause low ductility with consequent like-
llhood of rupturing the band in firing.
Dimensional tolerances on the finished shell, if taken advantage of to the full extent, may produce
shell which do not come within the weight tolerances. These large tolerances are made for the convenience of
the manufacturer, and he can decide which ones he wishes to decrease and the ones he wishes to take full ad-
vantage of so as to produce a shell that will come within the weight tolerances. The working gages, as shown
