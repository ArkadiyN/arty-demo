# Fragmentation Literature

## Collected Sources

### Cylinder Expansion Test & Gurney Equations

- **Fuchs (1995, DTIC ADA300526)** — Picatinny Arsenal Cylinder Expansion Test and Mathematical Examination of the Expanding Cylinder\
    Experimental methodology for measuring wall velocity via streak camera; develops modified Gurney equation accounting for wall thinning. Velocity evolution given implicitly via polynomial fit to measured position-time data.\
    \[[ada300526-picatinny-cylinder-test/](ada300526-picatinny-cylinder-test/)\]

- **Breech (2011, DTIC ADA540284)** — Extension of the Gurney Equations to Two Dimensions for a Cylindrical Charge\
    Theoretical derivation of 2D Gurney equations for cylindrical charge with top/bottom plates. Assumes uniform acceleration; provides linear velocity profiles during expansion in both radial and axial directions.\
    \[[ada540284-gurney-2d-extension/](ada540284-gurney-2d-extension/)\]

- **Martineau (1998, LANL LA-13424-T)** — A Viscoplastic Model of Expanding Cylindrical Shells Subjected to Internal Explosive Detonations\
    Experimental validation of viscoplastic shell-expansion model using copper cylinders (2.54 mm and 5.08 mm thick) with PBX-9501 charge. Compares Gurney predictions (2902 m/s for thin, 2351 m/s for thick) against numerical model and Fabry-Perot measurements. Radial velocity time-histories (Figures 5.7–5.8) and instability counts tabulated.\
    \[[martineau1998-viscoplastic-shell-expansion/](martineau1998-viscoplastic-shell-expansion/)\]

### Ballistic Penetration & Perforation

- **UFC 4-023-07 (2008)** — Design to Resist Direct Fire Weapons Effects\
    U.S. Army Corps of Engineers / NAVFAC / AFCESA Unified Facilities Criteria. Provides engineering design equations for ballistic and antitank weapon protection of structures. Equation 5-1 (wood perforation thickness via empirical curve fit) and Equation 5-2 (residual velocity after wood penetration) are the primary direct-fire ballistic perforation formulations referenced in literature. Table 5-5 catalogs material properties (hardness, density) for wood species under dry and wet conditions. Applies to small-arms and shoulder-fired antitank weapons (≤12.7 mm / 0.50 caliber).\
    \[[ufc-4-023-07-direct-fire-weapons-effects/](ufc-4-023-07-direct-fire-weapons-effects/)\]

- **Sanborn et al. (2019)** — Ballistic Performance of Cross-laminated Timber (CLT)\
    Experimental penetration-depth and residual-velocity data for 0.5 in. (12.7 mm) hardened S-2 tool-steel sphere projectiles (180–1200 m/s) fired into SPF-S and SYP CLT targets (3- and 5-ply, varying thicknesses). Classical (Euler–Robins, Poncelet, Resal) and empirical penetration models calibrated to data; Force Law and THOR-based models developed with recalibrated constants. Ballistic-limit perforation thickness models derived (Eq. 15–16). MSE comparison table (Table 9) ranks model accuracy; CLT THOR model (MSE=0.303) recommended for 400–3000 ft/s range, CLT thickness >4 in., similar-mass projectiles. Weathering effects (moisture) found negligible.\
    \[[sanborn2019-clt-ballistic-performance/](sanborn2019-clt-ballistic-performance/)\]
