# 75mm M48 HE — initial-condition check against ordnance-1944.md

Investigation only (no `src/arty/` changes). Checks whether the Family B
over-prediction of B(r) for the 75mm M48 (per
`b-vs-range.qmd`) traces to an input mismatch rather than a
field-spreading/geometry error.

## Source-stated values (`doc-reference/wound-ballistics/ordnance-dept-1944-shell-fragment-damage/ordnance-1944.md`)

- (a) Initial fragment velocity (V0): **3,120 f/s** (= 951.0 m/s), stated
    explicitly as a header line above Table 43/44
    ("INITIAL FRAGMENT VELOCITY 3,120 F/S", `ordnance-1944.md:383`).
- (b) Minimum effective / lethal fragment weight: **not a single constant** —
    Table 43 (CASUALTIES) gives a *per-range* "lightest effective fragment"
    weight `m(r)` [oz] and velocity `v(r)` [f/s] pair, e.g. r=20 ft:
    m=0.014 oz, v=2,060 f/s; r=100 ft: m=0.063 oz, v=972 f/s; r=400 ft:
    m=0.244 oz, v=494 f/s (`ordnance-1944.md:392-411`). Verified these are
    generated by a fixed casualty energy threshold: KE = 0.5·m·v² at r=20 ft
    → 0.014 oz (2.72e-5 slug) × 2060 f/s² / 2 ≈ 57.7 ft-lb, matching the
    card's stated 58 ft-lb casualty criterion (`card.md:25`,
    `ordnance-1944.md:309`). So the source's "minimum lethal mass" is
    velocity-coupled per range, not a fixed mass.
- (c) Fragment striking velocity at range: same Table 43 `v(r)` column above
    — this is the velocity of the *lightest effective* fragment specifically,
    not a single representative fragment's velocity-vs-range curve.

## Model-computed / model-used values (`arty.shells`, `arty.fragmentation`)

- (a) Gurney V0 for 75mm M48: `arty.fragmentation.gurney_velocity(SHELLS["75mm   M48 HE"])` = **807.5 m/s = 2,649 f/s** (Gurney const 2440 m/s for TNT,
    mass_shell=5.755 kg from mass_total 6.622 − mass_filler 0.6668 −
    mass_deductions 0.200, C/M = 0.1159). This is **15.1% lower** than the
    source-stated 3,120 f/s (951.0 m/s).
- (b) Minimum lethal fragment mass: `min_lethal_mass()` bisects on
    `ke_at_range()` for a *given* `E_leth`; the b-vs-range challenge already
    passes `E_leth=58 ft-lb ≈ 78.6 J` explicitly
    (`b-vs-range.qmd:91-92,119`), matching the source's
    threshold — **not** the module's own `E_LETH_DEFAULT=1000 J`
    (`fragmentation.py:439`, unused by this challenge). So the energy
    *threshold* is already matched; only the mass ↔ velocity mapping used to
    hit that threshold can differ (via V0 and the drag/retardation law).
- (c) Model velocity-vs-range: `ke_at_range` uses exponential decay
    `v(s) = V0 · exp(-λs)` with `λ = retardation_coeff(m)` depending on
    fragment mass (`fragmentation.py:220-227,257-267`) — i.e. velocity decay
    is fragment-mass-dependent, whereas the source table reports a single
    v(r) curve for *the lightest effective fragment at each r* (its mass
    changes with r too, so it is not a pure single-fragment decay curve
    either — see note below).

## Comparison

**(a) V0 — 15% low, wrong direction to explain over-prediction.** Model
Gurney V0 = 807.5 m/s vs source-stated 951.0 m/s (3,120 f/s): model is
**15.1% too low**. A too-low V0 makes fragments *less* energetic at every
range (lower N0 too, via `mott_params`'s `mu ~ (r_bu/V0)^3`), which would
bias the model toward *under*-predicting casualties, not over-predicting
them. This does not explain the observed over-prediction; if anything the
model over-predicts *despite* this offset working the other way.

**(b) Energy threshold — already matched, ruled out.** The b-vs-range
challenge already substitutes the card's 58 ft-lb (78.6 J) threshold for
`E_LETH_DEFAULT` (1000 J), so this input is correctly aligned with the
source and is not the cause.

**(c) Velocity-vs-range — large, range-growing discrepancy; best explanation
found.** Feeding the model's own `retardation_coeff(m)` the source's own
per-range lightest-effective-fragment mass `m(r)` and the source's own V0
(3,120 f/s), the model's predicted `v(r) = V0·exp(-λ(m)·s)` **overshoots
the source's reported v(r) by a growing factor**: ~1.2–1.4× at r=20-40 ft,
rising to **~3.0–3.5× at r=300-400 ft**. Equivalently, fitting a
range-local λ directly from the source's `(m(r), v(r))` pairs and V0 shows
the source's *implied* retardation coefficient is **~3–5.5× larger** than
`retardation_coeff` computes for that same fragment mass (ratio falls
gently from ~5.5× at 20 ft to ~3.2× at 400 ft, i.e. not a constant offset —
consistent with a systematically too-small drag term rather than a V0
scaling error). Concretely: model `λ(0.014 oz)` ≈ 0.0123 m⁻¹ vs. source-
implied ≈ 0.068 m⁻¹ at r=20 ft.

Because this ratio *grows with range*, under-decelerated model fragments
retain increasingly more kinetic energy than real fragments the farther out
you go — exactly the "growing with range" shape reported for the B(r)
over-prediction.

## Implication for the B(r) over-prediction

The most likely single driver of the reported 7–33× over-prediction
(growing with range) is **`retardation_coeff`'s drag term being too small**
for real fragments, not the V0 mismatch (wrong sign/too small to matter) or
the lethal-energy threshold (already matched). Under-decelerating fragments
means `min_lethal_mass(s, ...)` returns a mass that is systematically **too
low** at long range (less mass needed to still clear 78.6 J when the model
thinks velocity decayed less than it really does), so `mott_N` counts more
fragments as still-lethal than actually are — an effect that compounds with
range, matching the observed growth in over-prediction.

The likely candidate for the missing factor: `C_D=0.65` combined with
`C_shape=0.90` (`DragParams` defaults, `fragmentation.py:112-114`) describes
a fairly streamlined body. Irregular, tumbling steel fragments are commonly
reported with substantially higher effective drag (larger presented area
from tumbling/irregular shape); a combined `C_D·C_shape` of order 2–3
instead of the current 0.585 would close most of the observed ~3–5.5×
retardation gap. This is a plausible, testable hypothesis, not a confirmed
fix — quantifying and correcting `C_D`/`C_shape` (or the retardation-law
form itself) against the source's own fragment velocity-decay data is
follow-up derivation work, out of scope for this investigation pass.

**Not investigated further here (secondary, smaller effects):** the 15% V0
shortfall (wrong-direction fix candidate); whether Gurney's constant/C-M
ratio inputs for the 75mm M48 (mass_deductions=0.200 kg placeholder,
wall_t estimated) are individually accurate — these are minor next to the
order-of-magnitude drag-law gap.
