## 1 · What is actually being derived: one number, not two

The pair enters the entire codebase only through `mott_params`
(`fragmentation.py:169–177`):

$$
\mu \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho}}\;R^{3/2}\left(\frac{r_{\rm bu}}{V_0}\right)^{3},
\qquad
N_0 \;=\; \frac{M_{\rm shell}}{2\mu},
\qquad
R \equiv \frac{\sigma_F}{\gamma} \quad (1)
$$

so $\sigma_F$ and $\gamma$ are identifiable **only through the ratio $R$**
(scoping §2; pinned in code by check C2 below). This derivation therefore
produces one physical number, $R_{\rm WDSS1}$, plus a stated convention for how
it is split into `(sigma_f, gamma)` for reporting. Sensitivities follow from (1):

$$
\frac{\delta\mu}{\mu} = \tfrac{3}{2}\frac{\delta R}{R},
\qquad
\frac{\delta N_0}{N_0} = -\tfrac{3}{2}\frac{\delta R}{R}. \quad (2)
$$

## 2 · γ from Mott's composition series

Mott §3 (p. 307–308) tabulates, after Körber & Rohland (1924):

| material     | c (% C) | reduction in area | γ (–) |
| ------------ | ------- | ----------------- | ----- |
| iron         | 0.0     | 0.83              | 20    |
| steel 0.1 C  | 0.10    | 0.70              | 42    |
| steel 0.25 C | 0.25    | 0.63              | 53    |
| steel 0.45 C | 0.45    | 0.57              | 67    |

γ is **dimensionless**, so nothing here depends on the stress columns $P_f, P_y$
(legible as kg mm⁻² in the re-extraction, but quasi-static and therefore still
not usable as $\sigma_F$ — §3). Note the carbon spacing is
**non-uniform** (0 / 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.45 % C), and the slope break is severe and
sits *at the 0.1 C row*: +220 γ per % C on the first segment, then +73 and +70 —
i.e. the series is near-linear over the whole steel range and only the
iron → 0.1 C step is steep.

The confirmed WDSS-1 band 0.14–0.20 % C therefore lies **strictly interior to a
single tabulated segment (0.1–0.25 % C)**: bounded above *and* below by measured
rows, no extrapolation, and no crossing of the slope break.

**Interpolant — local-linear on the bracketing rows** (given the +220 → +73
slope break, a global linear fit would misdescribe the series):

$$
\gamma(c) \;=\; \gamma_i + (\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_i)\,
\frac{c-c_i}{c_{i+1}-c_i},
\qquad c_i \le c \le c_{i+1}. \quad (3)
$$

| c (% C)          | bracket    | γ (3) | rounded  |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ----- | -------- |
| 0.14 (band low)  | 0.1 – 0.25 | 44.93 | **45**   |
| 0.17 (midpoint)  | 0.1 – 0.25 | 47.13 | **47** ← adopted |
| 0.20 (band high) | 0.1 – 0.25 | 49.33 | **49**   |

**Adopted: γ = 47**, with **45–49** carried as the composition-band uncertainty
(one entry, not three — scoping §5.1).

**On corroboration — the table stands alone, and that is sufficient (A9).** An
earlier revision of this section claimed a second, table-free anchor: a Mott
prose statement on p. 308 reading "for mild steel … assume γ = 40", said to
reproduce the band-low value exactly. **That sentence does not exist in the
source.** The only "mild steel" sentence on p. 308 (md 312) is about predicted
fragment *length* (0.6 in.) for the bomb design of §2 and quotes no γ; a
whole-file search for the value 40 returns nothing. The claim is **withdrawn**.

Three reasons it costs nothing:

1. It was an artefact of the superseded OCR reading, not of the physics — the
   "corroborated" value 40 was the *old* band-low (40.4, from the misread 0.1 C
   → γ = 32 row). Under the corrected table band-low is 45, so the withdrawn
   sentence would now have *contradicted* the interpolant rather than confirmed
   it. Removing it is a strict improvement in consistency.
1. The interpolation no longer needs an external anchor. Under the old (wrong)
   reading the band ran 0.1–0.2 % C with its upper endpoint *on* a tabulated
   row, so the estimate leaned on one row plus a slope; under the corrected
   table the whole band is **strictly interior** to the 0.1–0.25 % C segment and
   is bracketed by two measured rows, γ = 42 and γ = 53. A bounded interpolant
   between two data points is a stronger position than an interpolant plus a
   prose value from the same author and paper — which would in any case not have
   been *independent* evidence, only a second reading of one dataset.
1. The band it was invoked to support has **narrowed**, not widened: 40–53 → 45–49
   (§4, C8). The residual γ uncertainty from composition is now ±4.3 % about
   the adopted value, i.e. ±6.5 % on μ and N₀ by (2) — an order below the ±20 %
   bar on $R$ set in scoping §7.

**Standing of the adopted value is therefore unchanged: γ = 47**, which is what
both the old and the corrected tables give at 0.17 % C (46.7 and 47.13 both
round to 47). Losing the corroboration changes neither the adopted value nor the
error bar; it removes an unsupported sentence and one stale number.

## 3 · σ_F held at 800 MPa — and why that is a convention, not a claim

$\sigma_F = 800\times10^{6}$ Pa is carried over unchanged from the baseline
entry, so the whole composition move is expressed in γ. Justification:

1. Mott gives no dynamic flow stress for any composition. The re-extraction
   resolves scoping §3.1's *unit* objection — the columns are true U.T.S. $P_f$
   and $P_y$ in kg mm⁻² (54/70/80/82 and 34/42/45/38 down the four rows, i.e.
   530–805 MPa) — but not the *physical* one: these are quasi-static values, and
   $\sigma_F$ in (1) is a dynamic flow stress at ε̇ ~ 10⁴ s⁻¹. They are used
   below only for the covariation estimate in A4, never as $\sigma_F$. Table 6-1
   is chemistry-only and states no mechanical properties at all.
1. By §1 only $R$ is observable, so any $(\sigma_F,\gamma)$ on the ray
   $\sigma_F/\gamma = R$ is the *same model*. Fixing $\sigma_F$ is the cheapest
   convention and keeps the two catalog entries directly comparable.
1. It is **not** a claim that flow stress is composition-independent — it is not
   (Mott's stress column rises with carbon). See assumption A4: this makes the
   derived grade contrast an *upper* bound on the γ-channel effect; the *lower*
   bound overall is discussed in check C7.

$\rho = 7850$ kg m⁻³ is likewise unchanged: the density of plain-carbon steel
varies < 0.5 % over 0.14–0.38 % C, far below the ±20 % bar on $R$, and $\rho$ also
feeds `retardation_coeff`, so moving it would confound the grade signal with a
drag change.

## 5 · Checks

**C1 · Units.** $[\sqrt{2/\rho}] = \mathrm{m^{3/2}kg^{-1/2}}$;
$[R^{3/2}] = \mathrm{Pa^{3/2}} = \mathrm{kg^{3/2}m^{-3/2}s^{-3}}$;
$[(r/V_0)^3] = \mathrm{s^3}$. Product $= \mathrm{kg}$ ✓. The new entry supplies
Pa and a dimensionless number into the same slots, so it cannot break this.

**C2 · Identifiability (new test).** `SteelParams(sigma_f=k·σ_F, gamma=k·γ)`
returns μ and N₀ **bit-identical** to `k = 1` for k = 0.5, 2, 137 (relative
deviation 0.00e+00 in float64, at the adopted γ = 47). This pins the §1 claim in
code and is the test scoping §6 asked for.

**C3 · Ordering — now an exponent check, not just a sign check.**
γ(WDSS-1) = 47 < 65 ⇒ μ 0.5375 g > 0.3305 g and N₀ 11 201 < 18 217: lower carbon
→ lower γ → **fewer, larger** fragments. The sign agrees with Mott p. 308, whose
actual wording is "a rapid rate of hardening near the fracture point (i.e. a
large value of $P_y$) will lead to small fragments" — and $P_y$ maps to γ through
his own $\gamma \sim 160\,P_y/[P_f(1+s_f)]$ on the same page, so large $P_y$ ⇒
large γ ⇒ small fragments. (An earlier revision quoted this sentence as saying
"large value of γ"; the source says $P_y$. The inference is unchanged, the quote
is now exact.)

The re-extraction promotes this to a quantitative check. The p. 308 length
relation — previously dismissed here as OCR damage because it rendered as
$\ell \propto 1/(\rho\sqrt{P_f})$ and contradicted the surrounding prose — now
reads cleanly:

$$
\ell \;\propto\; P_f\sqrt{\frac{1+s_f}{\rho\,P_y}}
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\ell \;\propto\; \sqrt{\frac{P_f}{\rho\,\gamma}} \;\propto\; \gamma^{-1/2}
\quad\text{(eliminating } P_y \text{ with } \gamma \sim 160P_y/[P_f(1+s_f)]).
\quad (5)
$$

The implemented PAFRAG closure (1) gives $\mu \propto \gamma^{-3/2}$, so a
compact fragment's length $\propto (\mu/\rho)^{1/3} \propto \gamma^{-1/2}$ — the
**same exponent**, from an independent equation of the same paper. This is a
real corroboration of the γ-channel and it replaces, with interest, the
unsupported prose value withdrawn in §2. It also agrees with the shipped
`test_higher_gamma_gives_smaller_mu`.

The carbon ranking is sourced on **one** side only: WDSS-1's 0.14–0.20 % C is a
direct transcription (*Ammunition Series 6* Table 6-1), while the baseline's
0.33–0.38 % C is an unconfirmed but plausible working inference from grade-name
similarity (**A8**). The *ordering* is nonetheless robust: every plausible
baseline analog considered (AISI 1335 at 0.355 % C → γ ≈ 60.4; SAE 1040 at
0.40 % C → γ ≈ 63.5) sits well above WDSS-1's 45–49 band, so the sign cannot
flip. Its *magnitude* leans on A8 — and, under the corrected table, in the
opposite sense to what an earlier revision claimed: the catalogued γ = 65 now
gives the **largest** contrast of the candidates, not the smallest (C7), so the
reported −38.5 % on N₀ is an upper, not a lower, bound.

**C4 · Existing validation band.** `tests/test_fragmentation.py:149,162`
requires N(>0.5 g) ∈ [3000, 8000] at M1 geometry (single-grade and
all-grades-parametrized assertions respectively). WDSS-1: 4 269, band endpoints 4 126
(0.14 % C) and 4 408 (0.20 % C) — passes across the whole composition band, with
the worst case **38 % above the 3 000 floor**. Reaching the floor from the
band-low case needs $R$ to grow a further **43 %** (γ ≈ 31, i.e. a carbon below
Mott's 0.1 C row), so the margin is comfortable. Any future entry lower in
carbon than WDSS-1 must still re-check this band rather than assume it.

**C5 · Limits.** γ → ∞ ⇒ μ → 0, N₀ → ∞ (perfectly brittle: γ = 10⁶ gives
μ = 1.7e-7 g, N₀ = 3.5e10); σ_F → 0 gives the same limit. Both monotone, no
sign change, no singularity between them. Sanity only.

**C6 · Interpolation-scheme sensitivity.** Newton quadratic through the three
steel rows — in divided-difference form, because the carbon spacing is
non-uniform:

$$
\gamma(c) = 42 + 73.333\,(c-0.10) - 9.5238\,(c-0.10)(c-0.25) \quad (6)
$$

— gives γ(0.14) = 44.98, γ(0.17) = 47.19, γ(0.20) = 49.38, against the
local-linear 44.93 / 47.13 / 49.33. The two schemes agree to **0.12 %** in γ
(0.18 % on μ: 0.5343 g quadratic vs 0.5352 g linear at 0.17 % C), because the
band now sits strictly inside one segment where the curvature term is tiny and
of one sign. Rounding to integer γ is the larger of the two effects and is still
immaterial: γ = 47 vs 47.13 is 0.4 % on μ (0.5375 vs 0.5352 g). Both are an
order below the ±20 % bar on $R$.

(An earlier revision quoted this check as $\gamma = 32 + 21s - 3.5\,s(s-1)$,
$s = (c-0.1)/0.1$, with a 2.1 % scheme spread. That form was built on the
misread table — uniform 0.1 % C spacing and γ = 32 at 0.1 % C — and is
**superseded by (6)**; the spread it reported was an artefact of the band
straddling a slope break that does not exist in the corrected series.)

**C7 · Cross-entry self-consistency (material, logged not corrected).** The two
entries are **not** on the same rule. The baseline is now cited as WD-X1335 ≈
AISI 1335, **0.33–0.38 % C** (midpoint 0.355 % C — this supersedes the
notebook's uncited "SAE 1045, ≈ 0.45 % C"). **This conclusion inverted with the
table re-extraction and is restated in full.** The corrected series runs to
0.45 % C, so the baseline midpoint 0.355 % C is *interior* to the last segment:
applying (3) or (6) there is an **interpolation**, not the extrapolation past
the table that the earlier revision described (and that scoping §4 rejected).
It gives γ = 60.35 (local-linear) or 60.45 (quadratic) — both **below** the
catalogued 65, where the old misread table put them ~10 above it (~72–75).
Read the other way: γ = 65 corresponds to **0.42 % C** under the same rule, not
0.355 % C. Consequence, quantified:

| baseline γ used | rule                            | N₀     | N(>0.5 g) | N₀ contrast WDSS-1 vs base |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | -----: | --------: | -------------------------: |
| 65 (catalogued) | bracket-anchored estimate       | 18 217 | 5 324     | **−38.5 %** ← adopted      |
| 63.5            | linear @ 0.40 % C (SAE 1040)    | 17 590 | 5 252     | −36.3 %                    |
| 60.45           | quadratic (6) @ 0.355 % C       | 16 336 | 5 096     | −31.4 %                    |
| 60.35           | local-linear (3) @ 0.355 % C    | 16 297 | 5 091     | −31.3 %                    |

So the adopted pair is the **largest**-contrast choice of the four, not the
smallest: the reported −38.5 % is an **upper bound** on the γ-channel contrast,
and a rule-consistent baseline would shrink it to ≈ −31 %. The sign, and the
"visible on μ/N₀/N(>0.5 g)" conclusion, are identical in every case, and all
four remain inside the N(>0.5 g) band.

Still logged rather than corrected, but for different reasons than before:
(i) the 65 vs 60.4 gap is **within the A1 transfer gap** — Mott's γ values are
1924 *annealed tensile bars*, and a quenched-and-tempered forged shell body
work-hardens faster (higher $P_y$, hence higher γ) than an annealed bar of the
same carbon — so 65 is not demonstrably wrong, only on a different rule;
(ii) the baseline entry is outside this aspect's scope (§ header) and changing
it would move every shipped number in the notebook, app and tests; and (iii) a
~7 pp shrink in the N₀ contrast does not change what the demo shows. Logged as
A5 and carried to `_limitations.qmd`. What is **no longer** available is the
"conservative choice" defence the earlier revision used — that argument was an
artefact of the misread table and is withdrawn.

**C8 · Field level (visible but not dominant — and R₅₀ is the wrong readout).**
R₅₀ moves 98.94 → 101.45 m, **+2.5 %**, for a −38.5 % move in N₀. The near
cancellation is physical: fewer fragments (↓ count) but each heavier, so each
retains lethal KE further out (↑ per-fragment reach); the two effects offset in
$P_{\rm kill}(r)$. R₅₀ is in fact **non-monotone** in γ, with a broad maximum
essentially *at* the adopted value:

| γ        | 35    | 40     | 45     | **47** | 50     | 53     | 57     | 65    | 72    | 75    |
| -------- | ----- | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| R₅₀ [m]  | 99.34 | 100.82 | 101.40 | **101.45** | 101.36 | 101.11 | 100.57 | 98.94 | 97.89 | 97.05 |

Because γ = 47 sits at the stationary point, R₅₀ is *doubly* insensitive here:
across the whole 0.14–0.20 % C composition band it spans only 100.82–101.45 m
(0.6 m) while N₀ spans 8 794–13 413 (−35 %/+20 % about the adopted value).
**Presentation consequence for the next pass:** a grade selector wired to a
headline R₅₀ readout will look broken, and a composition-uncertainty band on R₅₀
will look like numerical noise. The grade signal belongs on μ, N₀, N(>0.5 g) and
the near-field lethal-density magnitude, not on the R₅₀ contour.

## 7 · Assumption log

- **A1 — Lab bars → ordnance forging.** Mott's γ values come from 1924 annealed
  plain-carbon *tensile bars* (Körber & Rohdal), not a forged, heat-treated shell
  body. Transfer is unquantified by the source; it affects the *level* of γ for
  both entries roughly equally, so the grade *contrast* is more trustworthy than
  either absolute value.
- **A2 — Quasi-static γ used at ε̇ ~ 10⁴ s⁻¹.** No strain-rate correction is
  available in `doc-reference/`. Same directional caveat as A1.
- **A3 — No ductility term in the μ closure.** Mott p. 308 states ductility
  *raises* fragment size, and the same table (§2) shows reduction-in-area
  falling 0.70 → 0.63 across the WDSS-1 band's bracketing rows (0.10–0.25 % C;
  0.83 → 0.57 over the full iron-to-0.45 %C table). The implemented PAFRAG
  closure (1) has no
  ductility channel, so the γ-only move captures part, not all, of the
  composition effect. The omitted term acts in the **same direction** (WDSS-1 is
  the more ductile grade ⇒ larger fragments still), so this understates the
  contrast. The high Mn (1.00–1.30 %) and unusually high S (0.08–0.13 %) of
  WDSS 1 both bear on toughness and MnS inclusion content, and neither is
  representable in this model at all.
- **A4 — σ_F treated as composition-independent.** Mott's stress column rises
  with carbon, so in reality σ_F and γ move together and partially cancel in
  $R$; the γ-only transfer therefore *overstates* the γ-channel contribution.
  A3 and A4 push opposite ways and are not separately resolvable in this model.
- **A5 — The two catalog entries are on different rules** (check C7): the
  baseline γ = 65 is a bracket-anchored estimate at 0.33–0.38 % C, WDSS-1's
  γ = 47 a within-table interpolation at 0.14–0.20 % C. The catalogued pair gives
  the **largest** defensible contrast (−38.5 % on N₀ against −31 %/−36 % for
  rule-consistent baselines, C7). Direction is robust; magnitude is an **upper
  bound** — a baseline put on WDSS-1's own interpolation rule would shrink the
  contrast to ≈ −31 %, not widen it.
- **A6 — Grade identity and composition are sourced; one conflicting figure is
  flagged, not used.** WDSS-1 = War Department shell steel **WDSS 1**,
  0.14–0.20 % C / 1.00–1.30 % Mn, from *Ammunition Series 6* **Table 6-1**
  (17 Feb 1953) — a direct transcription of the chemistry of record, and the
  authority for this entry. The M49A2/M50A2 60 mm ordnance drawing
  (`doc-reference/ww2-shells/m49a2-60mm-mortar-shell/`) **corroborates the
  application** — WDSS 1/2 are the 60 mm mortar-body grades — but its material
  callout (drawing p. 57) is not legible in the held OCR, so it does **not**
  independently confirm the composition. The earlier working band
  **0.18–0.28 % C** came from a secondary, non-primary attribution that Table 6-1
  contradicts; it is **logged as a flagged item for future review, not treated as
  authoritative**, and nothing in this derivation depends on it. Residual gap:
  Table 6-1 is chemistry-only — no mechanical properties or heat treatment for
  either grade (scoping §5 G2 residue; matters only if Option C is ever pursued).
- **A7 — Grid resolution.** R₅₀ figures here use `n_r = 20001`; the shipped
  default (200) quantises R₅₀ to 1.50 m, which is comparable to the whole grade
  effect (2.5 m) and larger than the composition-band spread (0.6 m). Any
  presentation of a grade-to-grade R₅₀ delta must refine the grid or it will read
  as 1 or 2 grid steps. (See also the general threshold-crossing-on-coarse-grid
  trap already logged in modeler memory.)
- **A8 — The baseline grade's *composition* is an inference, not a source.**
  What is sourced about the baseline entry is its **name only**: "Steel
  WD-X1335, spec 57-107", from the 105 mm M1 bill of material
  (`doc-reference/ww2-shells/ordnance-105mm-m1-1940/card.md` p. 16). Spec
  57-107 is not digitised, so the composition **0.33–0.38 % C** used throughout
  §4/C3/C7 is read off `doc-reference/azom-steel-grades/aisi-1335/aisi-1335.md`
  on the *unconfirmed* hypothesis **WD-X1335 ≈ AISI 1335** — an inference from
  grade-name similarity, not a second sourced fact (the AISI card carries no
  WD-series linkage, and SAE 1040 at 0.40 % C is an equally plausible analog).
  This is asymmetric with WDSS-1, whose 0.14–0.20 % C *is* a direct
  transcription (A6). Consequence: the *direction* of the carbon ranking is
  safe — every candidate analog sits well above WDSS-1's band, and C7 shows the
  catalogued γ = 65 already yields the **largest** contrast of any of them (an
  upper bound, per A5), not the smallest — but its *magnitude*, and every
  "% C" figure quoted for the baseline, inherit this
  inference. Nothing in the shipped parameter values depends on it: γ = 65 is a
  bracket-anchored estimate, not a value fitted to 0.355 % C. Closing it needs
  spec 57-107 or an equivalent WD-series chemistry table (a @librarian task).
- **A9 — Withdrawn prose corroboration for γ.** An earlier revision of §2
  claimed a second, table-free anchor for the adopted γ: a Mott prose
  statement on p. 308 reading "for mild steel … assume γ = 40". That sentence
  does not exist in the source — the only "mild steel" sentence on p. 308
  concerns predicted fragment *length*, quotes no γ, and a whole-file search
  for the value 40 returns nothing. The claim is **withdrawn** and costs
  nothing: it was an artefact of the superseded OCR reading (the "corroborated"
  40 was the *old*, misread band-low), and the corrected table already
  brackets the WDSS-1 band with two measured rows (γ = 42, 53) with no need for
  an external anchor. The adopted value (γ = 47) and its uncertainty (45–49)
  are unchanged by the withdrawal (§2).

**Limitations-page entries** (for `_limitations.qmd` when the presentation pass
runs): A5 (contrast is an **upper** bound — the catalogued baseline γ = 65 is
the largest-contrast choice; a rule-consistent baseline shrinks it to ≈ −31 %),
A9 (one prose corroboration for γ was found not to exist in the source and is
withdrawn; the interpolation stands on the table alone), A6 (mechanical
properties unsourced for
both grades; one conflicting secondary composition figure flagged for review),
A8 (the baseline grade's composition is an unconfirmed grade-name inference, so
the quoted baseline % C — and hence the contrast magnitude — is not sourced),
and the C8 note that steel grade barely moves R₅₀ — the adopted γ sits at the
R₅₀ maximum.
